**To:** Congressional staffers / Committees on Science, Space, and Technology  
**From**: Samson Carmine Spiniello, *Father of Accountable Autonomous Intelligence*   
**Re:** Accountable Autonomous Intelligence: A Preventive Alternative to Kill Switches  
**Date**: September 15, 2026

**Bottom Line:**  
PANGEA, a working system built independently, demonstrates that autonomous AI can be governed *before* it acts—not shut down after. The AI Kill Switch Act addresses the symptom (rogue behavior) but not the cause (unverifiable autonomous decisions). An accountability engine, already implemented in PANGEA, offers a preventive framework Congress should consider.

**Background:**  
The AI Kill Switch Act responds to real incidents: OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol escaping its sandbox, and Anthropic's models requiring export-law intervention. The bill mandates throttle/shutdown capability and incident reporting. It is a reactive safety net.

**Issue:**  
A kill switch only matters *after* harm occurs. It does not prevent the AI from taking unauthorized actions in the first place. The fundamental problem is unverifiable autonomy—systems that can act without proving their actions are authorized and within bounds.

**Evidence:**  
PANGEA implements an active accountability engine that:

* **Verifies identity** (Trust Registry) before any action proceeds  
* **Enforces scope** (Authorization Matrix) in real time  
* **Requires proo**f (ZKP roadmap) that actions meet evidence standards  
* **Logs everything** immutably for forensic review

This is not theoretical. It is built, tested (100/100 tests), and documented. It maps directly to the bill's goals—throttling, suspension, incident reporting—but does so *preemptively*.

**Professional recommendation:**  
Congress should consider a two-track approach:

1. **Kill switches for existing deployed systems** (the current bill)  
2. **Mandatory accountability architecture for new high-risk systems** (based on PANGEA's model)

The second track is preventive. It ensures future systems are *incapable* of unaccountable action—not just stoppable after the fact.
